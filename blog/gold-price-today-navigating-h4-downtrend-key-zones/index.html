Understanding Liquidity and Narrow Bands

Market liquidity often clusters around obvious highs and lows where resting stop losses reside. Smart money routinely sweeps these prominent levels before initiating true directional moves. Because of this dynamic, our high-probability zones are purposefully placed just beyond those obvious highs and lows inside narrow, well-defined bands.

For today's session, the primary H1 buy zone sits between 4,089.0 and 4,092.7, with invalidation placed strictly below 4,081.65. On the sell side, H1 resistance is identified at 4,132.3 to 4,136.0 (invalid above 4,143.35) and a higher band at 4,165.2 to 4,168.9 (invalid above 4,176.19). Meanwhile, deeper H4 structural levels offer broader parameters, featuring a lower buy zone at 4,055.5 to 4,059.1 (invalid below 4,048.13) and upper sell zones stretching toward 4,211.5.

Strict Invalidation Rules via Hourly Closes

A zone is only as good as its invalidation rule. In professional technical analysis, a simple wick through a level does not invalidate a zone; true market structure shift requires confirmation. Our methodology relies strictly on an hourly candle close beyond the defined invalidation threshold to confirm that a zone has failed.

If price respects the narrow band and shows rejection signs upon testing, traders monitor the reaction. If an hourly candle closes past the boundary—such as dropping below 4,081.65 for the H1 buy setup—the thesis is immediately neutralized, protecting capital from runaway trends.
